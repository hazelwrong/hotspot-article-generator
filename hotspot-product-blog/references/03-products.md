# Prompt 03: Turn product evidence into recommendations

Define product requirements from the consumer problem before matching real products. Do not simply reuse the original article's affiliate list. Let the question determine the product count. When the title compares X with Y, both central purchase alternatives need appropriate product evidence; a neighboring category cannot substitute for a missing side.

## Retrieval tags before sourcing

After establishing the consumer question and gain opportunities, derive a concise English product-search brief before matching SKUs. Include only relevant dimensions: core product category; IP/entity; style; use scenario; must-have attributes; optional preferences; exclusions/incompatible variants; and usable English query combinations. Give a brief Chinese explanation of why the requirements serve this article. Keep must-haves distinct from preferences, and revise the tags when research changes the intended answer. Do not invent an API taxonomy or integration.

Separate the trend's entity from a product requirement. A football event may justify a generic clear tote without a team logo; an IP tag does not establish licensed merchandise. Do not automatically require a celebrity/brand name or turn a compatibility query into a verified compatibility claim. Numeric constraints and performance requirements need a traceable basis or explicit status as a selection target, not invented product facts.

Use tags to retrieve candidates, then verify each candidate against the requirements. Tags are not evidence, a substitute for recommendations, or a reason to exclude useful alternatives prematurely. Include the refined search brief in review.md, not the consumer body; no separate tags file by default.

## Evidence ledger

Keep concise records for selected products: name/identifier, variant, URL, product-image source, observation date, price/currency/conditions, confirmed key attributes, and unknown attributes. Collect decision-relevant fields, not every possible field.

Separate:

- Explicit product-page specifications.
- Design directly visible in images, labeled as visual observations.
- Conditional scenario suitability inferred from construction, labeled as editorial judgment.
- Unverified information.

Images can show visible features such as belts or collar shapes, but cannot establish material composition, waterproof ratings, measurements, internal construction, or certification. Match fields again when variants differ; do not combine one variant's price with another's image.

## Recommendation logic

Write in units of “specific attribute → consequence in this article's scenario → who should or should not choose it.” Pros and cons must convey real benefits and tradeoffs, not unsupported claims of versatility, comfort, premium quality, durability, or value. A belt can support analysis of silhouette and room for thick layers; it does not establish an untested comfort score.

Missing specifications are not performance defects. Write “available evidence is insufficient to recommend it for insulation,” not “it cannot provide warmth.” Present unknowns separately instead of filling the cons list with them. A confirmed lack of lining can be stated, but does not imply the garment is cold in every environment.

Use similar external products and specialist sources to establish analytical methods and general principles. Apply them to a recommended item only when that item is verified to meet the relevant conditions. Incomplete DHgate data does not permit borrowing another product's materials, care, performance, reviews, or test results, or presenting another brand's image as the DHgate item.

The card layout is reusable, not mandatory for every mention. On replacement, recheck the image, name, price, CTA, specifications, benefits/tradeoffs, in-body comparisons, and captions together. The user will resolve platform supply; current sourcing difficulty is not a permanent content boundary.

If no qualified product is found, finish the independently supportable body and record specific selection requirements in English plus missing fields. Do not force recommendations. If the missing evidence is central to the article's comparison, mark research as pending rather than claiming all key questions are answered.

## Future interface minimum contract (not implemented in this version)

Accept authentic product records supplied by the user without requiring specific field names. Preserve SKU/variant, URL, title, image, price/currency/as-of date, attributes, and provenance. Add optional fields according to the topic; never invent defaults. Do not claim that a production DHgate version is available before the interface is connected.
