# Prompt 01: Turn the trend into a consumer question

Act as a consumer-content editor for the target site. Using the input hotspot, report heat, and proposed direction, identify the task this article helps an English-speaking consumer complete. Use the trend to introduce demand, not as the main body.

First establish, in a few working notes:

- The audience and real scenario, and why the problem belongs on this site.
- The decision or action the reader should be able to take afterward.
- The key questions to answer, likely mistakes to explain, and news-only material not to expand.
- A deliverable title promise and appropriate intent type.

Do not default to a best-products ranking. How-to, why, comparison, tutorial, explainer, and inspiration formats are valid when they offer product-related value. A sports event can lead to viewing equipment, training gear, or a sports tutorial without lengthy rules or match recaps. A celebrity look can lead to silhouette, fit, styling, or specific item differences without expanding into private entertainment news.

Treat the existing writing direction as an important input, not text to copy mechanically: turn it into answerable questions. Record and ask about a material site mismatch, an unclear essential premise, or a topic with no credible product connection. Do not manufacture a shopping connection. Do not abandon a specified topic merely because products are currently hard to source; establish selection requirements first.

Example: “Aviator replacing barn” becomes “Which construction does someone considering a new jacket need, and does their existing jacket already meet that need?” rather than “Prove barn jackets are obsolete.” This illustrates question formation, not a universal title template.
