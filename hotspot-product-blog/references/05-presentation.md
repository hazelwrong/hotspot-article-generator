# Prompt 05: Product and image integration

Deliver a readable, complete English HTML page. Reuse the sample's effective presentation principles without requiring its exact appearance or structure.

## Navigation and reading

For multiple sections, place a clickable table of contents near the opening. Match anchors to real headings and prevent mobile overflow. Use one H1, then H2/H3 sections. Include substantive sections and any product section that actually exists; do not create a product section solely for the contents list.

## Product cards

Introduce products where the reader needs a concrete example or choice, not as an unrelated pile at the end. Cards may use `.product-card`, `.product-grid`, and `.tradeoffs` with the CSS asset. Aim to include for each real recommendation:

- Selection purpose and product name, without invented “Editors' Choice” or “Best Overall” awards.
- The matching product image and concise caption or source.
- Actual price/currency and observation date; omit unverified prices instead of inventing numbers.
- A few decision-relevant specifications.
- An explicit CTA linking to the exact SKU/variant.
- Specific pros/cons, suitable and unsuitable scenarios, and necessary evidence limits.

These are information requirements, not a fixed card skeleton. Benefits and costs can appear in a relevant step, scenario paragraph, compact callout, or comparison row. Use full cards when they help the decision; do not force two adjacent cards or duplicate every recommendation in a separate Pros/Cons grid. Preserve useful visual product detail without letting the component determine the article outline.

Do not copy rating bars, awards, or test conclusions from reference screenshots. Subsequent prose should explain differences from alternatives, not repeat the table and pros/cons. When an attribute appears in both specifications and pros, the pro must explain its scenario consequence.

## Image purpose

New visuals are optional. Add them when they clarify silhouette, outfits, steps, dimensional relationships, or port positions; there is no quota. The body must independently answer the reader's task. Missing new illustrations do not block body acceptance; wrong existing images or references to nonexistent images still require correction or an explicit unresolved note. Explain what readers should notice and how to apply it; “look inspiration” alone is insufficient.

When the user explicitly authorizes editorial/public product images for local mockups, work within that authorization and retain traceable URLs and attribution. Otherwise use user-provided or appropriately licensed imagery, or omit optional new images. Public accessibility alone is not permission. Inspect images before describing them; do not invent details from filenames. Product images must match the actual product, and conceptual/style images must not masquerade as recommended items. Respect remote-media tool policies; do not download images to bypass display restrictions. If suitable images are unavailable, note this in Chinese review notes; do not fabricate visuals or default to image generation.

Do not display “Editorial image placeholder for this draft” or similar internal notes in the article. Do not independently claim verified permission from the rights holder. A user's local-mockup authorization does not establish production rights; follow the user's separately configured production image policy when supplied.

Use descriptive alt text, preserve aspect ratios, switch mobile cards to a single column, allow wide tables to scroll horizontally, and give buttons/links clear text. Scope styles to this article without changing other site templates. The HTML must open independently; document external-image dependencies in the Chinese notes.
